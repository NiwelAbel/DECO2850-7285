## Purpose of This Document

The team's Week 3 studio session produced a working problem scope (financial-difficulty segmentation, six sub-problems, early solution ideas). That work was real and valuable, but it existed only as paper notes and Miro brainstorming — undocumented, unverified, and not yet distinguished from assumption. This document formalises that thinking into a single reviewable baseline, explicitly marking what's assumed versus what's evidenced, so Phase 2 (Knowledge Gathering) has a clear target: validate, revise, or discard each assumption below using real data.

**This document is the gate between Phase 1 and Phase 2.** Phase 2 does not start in earnest until this document exists in reviewable form.

## 1. Research Question

**Primary:** How does parental absence affect left-behind children's emotional wellbeing, guidance, and development — and what is actually needed to address this gap?

| # | Sub-question | Lens |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | Who is affected, directly and indirectly, by a parent's labour migration? | Who |
| RQ2 | What forms does the separation take (duration, distance, communication frequency, caregiving arrangement)? | How |
| RQ3 | What economic, social, or structural factors drive the decision to migrate? | Why |
| RQ4 | What downstream effects are observed on wellbeing, academic outcomes, and family relationships? | Impact |
| RQ5 | Where might the team's assumptions about "left-behind children" as a uniform group break down? | Risks |

### Who
- Left-behind children (primary, but not directly researched — see Ethics section)
- Migrant parent(s)
- Remaining caregiver(s) — often grandparents, per the Week 3 security hypothesis
- Teachers, community workers, extended family

### Why
- Primary driver assumed to be economic necessity / labour migration **[ASSUMPTION]**

### Impact
- Emotional/psychological effects (unnoticed distress) **[ASSUMPTION]**
- Educational/value-shaping effects **[ASSUMPTION]**

## 5. Ethical Considerations

- No direct research contact with children — all primary data collection is with adults (caregivers, educators, community members) or secondary/public sources.
- Sensitive topic (family separation, child safety) — informed consent, anonymity, and the right to withdraw apply to all interview participants.
- The "security/kidnapping" hypothesis in particular needs careful, non-sensationalised handling in both research questions and any future public-facing materials.
- Ethics will be formally revisited in Week 4 (studio topic) and again after Phase 2 data collection.
