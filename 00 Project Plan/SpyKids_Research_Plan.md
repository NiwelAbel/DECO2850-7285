# SpyKids — Research Plan

**Topic:** Left-behind children (parental absence due to labour migration)
**Course:** DECO2850/DECO7285 Studio 2 — Interaction Design, Semester 2 2026
**Phase:** Problem Identification (no design/solution specification at this stage)

---

## 1. Purpose

This research plan exists to ground the team's understanding of the problem space in evidence rather than assumption, before any interaction design work begins. It does not propose a solution — its sole output is a well-evidenced problem framing that will feed Assessment 1 (individual proposal) and the team's ongoing design journal.

---

## 2. Research Questions

**Primary research question:**
How does parental absence affect left-behind children's emotional wellbeing, guidance, and development — and what is actually needed to address this gap?

**Sub-questions to guide data collection:**

| # | Sub-question | Maps to framing heading |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | Who is affected, directly and indirectly, by a parent's labour migration? | Who |
| RQ2 | What forms does the separation take (duration, distance, communication frequency, caregiving arrangement)? | How |
| RQ3 | What economic, social, or structural factors drive the decision to migrate? | Why |
| RQ4 | What downstream effects are observed on wellbeing, academic outcomes, and family relationships? | Impact |
| RQ5 | Where might the team's assumptions about "left-behind children" as a uniform group break down? | Risks |

---

## 3. Target Information Sources & Stakeholders

Because direct research contact with children is ethically sensitive and likely outside the scope/timeline of this studio, prioritise indirect and secondary sources first, escalating to direct contact only where appropriate and low-risk.

Assessment 2 sets a hard requirement here: **each team member must personally engage at least one participant using a specific method**, and the team as a whole should reach **6–10 participants** across the study. This isn't optional scoping — it's graded criteria for the Weeks 3–5 milestone.

| Source type | Examples | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Secondary literature | Peer-reviewed studies on migration and child development, NGO/government reports (e.g. UNICEF, ChildFund) | High — start here, supports but doesn't replace the participant requirement |
| Adults with lived proximity to the issue | Teachers, school counsellors, extended-family caregivers, community workers | High — main source for the 6–10 participant target |
| Online communities | Forums, support groups, social media spaces where migrant parents/caregivers discuss the experience | Medium |
| Object-based/artefact methods | Analysis of existing tools/apps already used by these families (e.g. video call apps, remittance apps) | Medium |
| Peers with relevant personal/family experience | Team members' own networks, only with informed and voluntary participation | Low — sensitive, opt-in only |

## 4. Suite of Methods (per Week 3 studio taxonomy)

Week 3's studio activity was specifically "Develop a Suite of Methods for your project," using the following taxonomy. Rather than the generic method list drafted earlier, the team's actual suite should be built from this set, with **one method assigned per team member** so everyone independently engages at least one participant (Assessment 2 requirement).

| Method | Type | What it involves | Best fit for this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contextual Inquiry | Ethnography | Combined interview + in-field observation; go in without preconceived assumptions, observe and learn in-situ (master/apprentice or partnership mode) | Observing a caregiving household or classroom setting, if access allows |
| Rapid Ethnography | Ethnography | Quicker, focused version for limited time/budget — narrow focus, key informants, multiple observers, cooperative analysis | Time-boxed sessions with teachers/community workers given the studio's tight timeline |
| Digital Ethnography | Ethnography | Immersing in an online community (forums, Facebook groups, Reddit), following ethical conduct, informing the community/gatekeeper, observing and optionally participating | Migrant-parent or caregiver support forums/groups |
| Mapping | Object-based | Ask a participant to draw a map (e.g. relationships, routines, communication patterns) then question them about it: "why did you emphasise this?", "how are these connected?" | Mapping a family's communication routine across distance |
| Object Inventory | Object-based | Use physical/digital objects in a participant's environment as interview prompts — their history, intention, use | Communication tools/objects a family already relies on (phone, calling app, letters) |
| Autoethnography | Reflexive | Team member studies the environment through their own experience, using reflexivity to connect it to wider social/cultural meaning — not a diary, avoid self-indulgence | Only if a team member has directly relevant lived experience; use cautiously and opt-in |

**Suggested method-to-member assignment (fill in with actual names):**

| Team member | Assigned method | Target participant(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Member 1 | Contextual Inquiry / Rapid Ethnography | 1+ |
| Member 2 | Digital Ethnography | 1+ (observation-based, may not need 1:1 contact) |
| Member 3 | Mapping | 1+ |
| Member 4 | Object Inventory | 1+ |
| Member 5 | Contextual/Rapid Ethnography (2nd instance) | 1+ |
| Member 6 | Digital Ethnography or Mapping (2nd instance) | 1+ |

This gives 6+ participants minimum, within the 6–10 range Assessment 2 expects, while covering the method diversity the studio taught.

## 5. Ethical Considerations

Assessment 2 explicitly requires the team to "add ethical insights and principles in your approach" — this needs to be documented evidence, not just an internal team norm.

- **No direct research contact with children.** All primary data collection is with adults (caregivers, educators, community members) or via secondary/public sources.
- **Informed consent** for any interview: participants told the purpose (student coursework), that participation is voluntary, and that they can withdraw at any time.
- **Anonymity/confidentiality:** no identifying details in journal entries, proposals, or exhibits; online ethnography examples paraphrased and not attributed to identifiable individuals.
- **Digital ethnography conduct:** per the studio's guidance, notify the online community/gatekeeper about the research before observing or participating, and understand the community's own norms before posting or engaging.
- **Sensitivity:** family separation is an emotionally sensitive topic; interview questions should be piloted within the team first, and interviewers should avoid pressing on distressing details.
- **Revisit ethics after data collection** (per Week 4 studio topic), not just before — check whether anything gathered raises issues not anticipated at the design stage.
- **Document as you go:** capture a short ethical-insight note alongside each piece of evidence (e.g. "this participant asked not to be quoted directly — respected by paraphrasing only") so the documentation itself satisfies Assessment 2's requirement.

## 6. Data Analysis Approach

- Qualitative thematic analysis (per Week 5 studio topic) on interview notes and ethnography logs.
- Code data against the five framing headings (Who/How/Why/Impact/Risks) plus an "unexpected" category to catch findings that don't fit the existing frame.
- Synthesis session as a team to reconcile findings across methods and update the assumptions list.

## 7. Timeline (aligned to Assessment 2's official Weeks 3–5 process)

| Week | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Week 3 | Literature review; finalise Suite of Methods and per-member assignment; draft assumptions; begin ethics prep | Annotated bibliography, method-to-member assignment, assumptions list |
| Week 4 | Each member conducts their assigned method with at least one participant; ethics re-check (Week 4 studio topic) as data comes in | Interview transcripts / observation logs / maps / object inventories from each member — evidence of individual engagement |
| Week 5 | Complete remaining participant engagements (target 6–10 total); qualitative data analysis (Week 5 studio topic); team synthesis session | **A design concept based on data analysis** (Assessment 2's explicit Week 3–5 output) + updated Who/How/Why/Impact/Risks framing |
| Weeks 6–7 | Design concept feeds into individual Assessment 1 proposals and Iteration 1 prototyping | Individual proposals (07/09/26), early prototype work |
| Week 9 onward | Research resumes in evaluative form (user enactment, field trials) once a prototype exists | Evaluation data (separate from this problem-identification research plan) |

**Important nuance:** Assessment 2 expects a team-level "design concept" to emerge by the end of Week 5 — earlier than the individual Assessment 1 proposal (due Week 7) might suggest. This isn't a full design specification; it's the point where the *team's* data analysis should start pointing toward a shared direction, which each member then individually develops and argues for in their own Assessment 1 proposal. Keep the Week 5 "concept" at the level of a direction/hypothesis, not a specified solution.

## 8. Limitations & Risks

- **Access:** the team may have limited direct access to people with first-hand experience; secondary sources and online ethnography should be weighted accordingly.
- **Small sample size:** interview findings are illustrative, not statistically representative — avoid overgeneralising.
- **Risk of assuming uniformity:** explicitly test (not assume) that the experience is similar across age groups, geographies, and family structures — this is the flagged risk in the team's existing framing and should be a standing check at each synthesis session.
- **Emotional sensitivity:** be prepared for interviews or ethnography to surface distressing content; agree in advance how the team will handle this respectfully.
